Reporting is not warning.
Headroom is.

Jabil’s Credit Management Dashboard reports credit exposure as it stands today, faithfully and only as it stands. It cannot say which customer will pass a limit next month, which one is quietly getting worse, or why. Headroom can — from SAP data Jabil already has, plus public information anyone can read.

Advice is not automation. It is the whole design.

A credit tool earns its place by what it refuses to do. These four refusals are built into the software, not written into a policy document beside it.

01

It reads. It never writes.

Headroom reads from SAP and writes nothing back. No limit is changed, no order is held, no customer is contacted. The connector is read-only by construction, and there is no write path to misuse.

02

A number without a reason is not usable.

Every driver adds or removes percentage points, so the reasons on screen always sum to the score. If the tool cannot say why, it does not say the number — and the model’s constants are printed on the screen rather than buried in it.

03

Each customer is judged against itself.

A customer paying four days late is behaving normally if four days is its normal. Every measure is compared with that account’s own twelve-week baseline, never with a peer group, so size and sector stop standing in for risk.

04

Growth is not distress.

A healthy customer can pass its limit while paying perfectly well. Conflating that with trouble is how a credit tool costs a company money, so the two questions are asked separately and Growth is its own zone, with a limit review rather than an order hold.

Four modules. One question.

Jabil asked for six capabilities. They arrive as four modules, each owned end to end by one person, each answering a different part of the same question: who needs attention, and what should be done.

Exposure forecast

Where exposure is heading over ninety days, and the chance, likely date and size of a limit breach.

Answers predict breaches

Early-warning score

Which customers are deteriorating and staying that way, measured against their own recent behaviour.

Answers risk scores, deterioration

External radar

Filings, official events, market signals and news tone, matched to the right legal entity before use.

Answers external credit information

Watchlist and reasons

A short ranked list every morning, each name carrying its drivers and one suggested next step.

Answers watchlists, explainability

We publish the months where it fails.

The back-test rolls forward monthly: train on everything known at a cut-off, then test only on the month that follows. A heavier model replaces a simpler one only if it wins on the same months at the same false-alarm rate.

One layer does not clear that bar. The external radar adds a few points, which sits inside the range of the layer below it, so it is marked not proven and kept only for the groups with SEC filings. That result is on the screen, not in a footnote.
